## Chapter 5: AI in the Wild: Applications, Ethics & Your Next Steps — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Name three industries where AI is currently being applied | Healthcare (diagnosis, drug discovery), Finance (fraud detection, trading), Transportation (autonomous vehicles, route optimization), Retail (recommendations, inventory), Manufacturing (quality control, predictive maintenance) |
| 2 | What is bias in AI systems? | Systematic errors in AI outputs that unfairly favor or disadvantage certain groups, often reflecting biases present in training data or algorithm design |
| 3 | What are the main current limitations of AI? | Lack of true understanding/common sense, brittleness outside training domains, inability to explain decisions, high data/compute requirements, no general intelligence |
| 4 | What does "responsible AI" mean? | Developing and deploying AI systems that are fair, transparent, accountable, safe, and respect privacy while minimizing harm to individuals and society |
| 5 | Why is Python recommended as a first programming language for AI/ML? | Beginner-friendly syntax, extensive ML libraries (scikit-learn, TensorFlow, PyTorch), large community support, and industry standard for data science |
| 6 | How can bias enter an AI system? | Through biased training data, biased feature selection, biased labeling, developers' unconscious biases, or feedback loops that amplify existing inequalities |
| 7 | What is an example of AI in healthcare? | Medical image analysis (detecting tumors in X-rays/MRIs), drug discovery, predicting patient readmission risk, or personalized treatment recommendations |
| 8 | What is algorithmic transparency? | The ability to understand and explain how an AI system makes its decisions, allowing for accountability and trust |
| 9 | What is a hands-on ML project good for beginners? | Titanic survival prediction, handwritten digit recognition (MNIST), spam email classification, or house price prediction |
| 10 | Why can AI systems be "brittle"? | They perform well only within their training distribution but fail unexpectedly when encountering slightly different real-world scenarios |
| 11 | What ethical concern arises with facial recognition AI? | Privacy violations, potential for mass surveillance, higher error rates for certain demographic groups, and misuse by authoritarian regimes |
| 12 | What is the "black box" problem in AI? | Complex AI models (especially deep learning) make decisions that are difficult or impossible for humans to interpret or explain |
| 13 | Name a way to mitigate bias in AI systems | Diverse and representative training data, bias audits, diverse development teams, regular testing across demographic groups, fairness constraints in algorithms |
| 14 | What is AI's role in autonomous vehicles? | Perception (detecting objects), decision-making (navigation choices), prediction (anticipating other drivers' behavior), and control (steering, braking) |
| 15 | What should be your next learning steps after understanding AI concepts? | Learn Python basics, study statistics/linear algebra fundamentals, complete hands-on ML tutorials, build small projects, and explore frameworks like scikit-learn |

### Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| AI Bias | Systematic prejudice in AI outputs resulting from flawed assumptions in training data or algorithm design |
| Responsible AI | Framework for developing AI that prioritizes fairness, accountability, transparency, and ethical considerations |
| Algorithmic Transparency | The degree to which an AI system's decision-making process can be understood and explained |
| Black Box Model | An AI system whose internal workings are opaque and difficult to interpret |
| Fairness in AI | Ensuring AI systems treat all individuals and groups equitably without discrimination |
| AI Brittleness | The tendency of AI to fail when encountering situations outside its training data |
| Explainability | The ability of an AI system to provide human-understandable reasons for its outputs |

### Memory Tricks
- **FATE** for responsible AI principles: **F**airness, **A**ccountability, **T**ransparency, **E**thics
- **"Garbage In, Garbage Out" (GIGO)** — Remember that biased training data leads to biased AI outputs
- **Python = "Pet snake"** — Just like a pet, Python is friendly to beginners but powerful when trained (like ML models!)
